# Items and widgets

This guide is about what sits on a dock: apps, folders, groups, the running apps, the clock, the weather, the tray, notifications and widgets. It says how to add each one, how to change a dock right on the dock, what each item does and where its settings are.

## What a dock holds

A dock holds a row of items, in the order you choose. An item can be:

- **An app**: a pinned app, shortcut or program.
- **A folder**: opens its contents in a popup.
- **A group**: several shortcuts in one slot, like a folder on a phone.
- **Running apps**: the place where apps that are running but not pinned appear.
- **A separator**: a thin line between parts of the dock.
- **The Recycle Bin**.
- **A clock**, with a month calendar.
- **Weather**, with a forecast.
- **The system tray**: the notification area icons.
- **The notification centre**: a bell with the list of your notifications.
- **A widget**: a small program such as volume, now playing or a system monitor.

You can have as many clocks, weather items and widgets as you like, each with its own settings. A dock has at most one running apps item, one tray and one notification centre.

## Adding items

Everything is added from the drawer: a grid of tiles of everything you can put on a dock, with a live preview of each widget. The drawer is in two places, and it is the same in both:

- Beside the dock while you customize it: click **Add items** on the small bar, or a **+** on the dock. See [Customize a dock](#customize-a-dock).
- On the **Contents** tab of the dock's settings, under **Add to the dock**.

To add an item:

1. Open the drawer.
2. Find the item. Type in the search box, or click a chip to show only part of the grid: **All**, **Built in**, **Widgets** or **Apps and folders**.
3. Click its tile to add it at the end of the dock. Or drag the tile onto the dock (or onto the Contents tab's strip) to the place you want: a marker shows where it will go.

The drawer holds:

- **App or shortcut…** and **Folder…**: they open a file dialog. Pick one or more apps or shortcuts, or a folder. These two are clicked, not dragged.
- The built-in items: Running apps, Clock, System tray, Notification centre, Recycle Bin and Separator.
- Every installed widget, such as **Weather (script)**, **Analog clock** or **System monitor**, each with a small live preview.

A dock can have only one Running apps item, one tray, one notification centre and one Recycle Bin. While it has one, that tile says "On the dock".

To put a widget on the desktop instead of on the dock, drag its tile onto the desktop, or press the small monitor button on its tile. See [Widgets on the desktop](#widgets-on-the-desktop).

There are quicker ways for some items:

- Drag a shortcut, a program or a folder from File Explorer onto the dock.
- Right-click the dock and open **Add**: **Clock**, **Weather (script)**, **Running apps**, **Tray (notify icons)**, **Notification centre** or **Separator**. **Apps, folders and more widgets…** opens the Contents tab, and **Folder window on the desktop…** makes a folder window.

To remove an item, customize the dock and click the item's **×**, or select it on the Contents tab and click **Remove** (or press Delete). A clock, a weather item, a widget, the tray and the notification centre also have **Remove this widget** at the end of their right-click menu. You can also drag a pinned app that is not running up and off the dock. A script widget dragged off the dock is not removed: it moves to the desktop (see [Widgets on the desktop](#widgets-on-the-desktop)).

## Customize a dock

Customizing lets you change a dock right where it is: add, move and remove its items, and move the dock itself. Every change applies at once and is one step you can undo.

To start customizing, do one of these:

- Right-click an empty part of the dock and choose **Customize this dock** (the first entry, after **Undo** when there is something to undo).
- In the dock's settings panel, click **Customize** in the header.
- Press and hold an empty part of the bar for a moment, without moving the mouse.

A locked dock asks first: "Unlock this dock to customize it?" Click **Unlock** to go on, or **Cancel**. Undo locks it again.

While you customize:

- A dashed outline in the dock's colour goes around the dock. The dock stays shown, and its icons do not grow.
- Point at an item: a small **×** appears at its corner. Click it to remove the item. There is no question; Undo brings it back.
- Drag an item to move it. A gap opens where it will land.
- While you drag an item, three small targets show by the bar: at its start, in the middle and at its end. Drop the item on one to give it that place of its own (start, centre or end), as **Position on the dock** does on the Contents tab.
- A **+** after your pinned apps and at the end of the dock opens the drawer. A dock with anchored items, a ring or a dock that scrolls has no **+**; use **Add items** there.
- Drag an empty part of the bar to move the dock itself. See [Moving a dock](guide-docks.md#moving-a-dock).

A small bar beside the dock (above a bottom dock, at its end) shows "Customizing" and the dock's name, the save state (**✓ Saved**) and these buttons:

- **Undo**: undoes the last change. **Ctrl+Z** does the same on the dock, and **Ctrl+Y** redoes it.
- **Add items** (or **Hide items** while it is open): shows or hides the drawer beside the dock.
- **Settings**: opens the dock's settings panel.
- **Done**: ends customizing.

To stop customizing, click **Done**, press **Esc**, choose **Done customizing** in the dock menu, or click another app or the desktop. After **Done** or **Esc**, the window you were using before is in front again.

## Right-click menus

Right-click an item and its menu starts with the item's name and icon, then what the item does, then how it looks, then its settings, and ends with removal. The last entry, **Dock**, holds the dock menu: the same menu you get by right-clicking an empty part of the dock. The menus show a small icon beside their main entries, the same icon for the same command in every menu.

- An app: **Open** or **New window**, **Open file location**, **Close** for each window, **Customize icon**, then **Pin to dock** or **Unpin from dock**.
- A folder: **Open in Explorer**, **Show contents**, **Contents layout**, **Customize icon**, **Folder settings…**, then **Unpin from dock**.
- A group: **Show contents**, **Rename…**, **Contents layout**, **Customize icon**, **Group settings…**, then **Ungroup (shortcuts stay pinned)** and **Unpin group and its shortcuts**.
- The weather: **Widget: Weather** at the top, **Refresh weather**, **Show forecast**, **Widget settings…**, then **Remove this widget**.
- The clock: **Widget: Clock** at the top, **Show calendar**, **Show seconds** and **Show date**, **Widget settings…**, then **Remove this widget**.
- A widget: **Widget:** and its name at the top (such as **Widget: System monitor**), **Widget settings…**, **Reload widget**, then **Remove this widget**.
- The tray and the notification centre: **Widget: Tray** or **Widget: Notification centre** at the top, **Show notification centre** (the notification centre), **Widget settings…**, then **Remove this widget**.
- A separator: **Remove separator**.

Some widgets put their everyday settings straight into this menu, above **Widget settings…**: an on / off setting is an entry with a check mark (click it to switch), a setting with a few values is a submenu with the current value marked (such as **Units** on the weather widget). The clock has **Show seconds** and **Show date**. A change made there applies at once, is saved, and can be undone like any other (**Undo** in the dock menu names it, such as "Changed Show seconds of Clock").

The dock menu is grouped by task. First, while there is something to undo, **Undo** with the name of the last change. Then for this dock: **Customize this dock**, **Dock settings…**, **Add**, **Position on screen**, **Auto-hide**, **Lock dock**, **Rename dock…**, **Duplicate dock** and **Save dock as layout…**. Then **All docks** (the dashboard, lock all), **App** (App settings, the taskbar, Start with Windows, Quick setup), **Tools** (the config file, widgets, Task Manager), **Help…** and **Quit**.

The WinDock icon in the notification area has the app-wide commands: **App settings…**, **Docks** (each dock's settings), **Show dashboard**, **Show Windows taskbar temporarily**, **Hide taskbar**, **Start with Windows**, **Tools**, **Help…** and **Quit**.

To move an item, drag it on the dock, or drag its tile on the Contents tab. To give an item a fixed place, such as the right end, drop it on a target while customizing, or use **Position on the dock** in the Contents tab's inspector.

## Item settings windows

A clock, a weather item, a widget, the tray, a folder and a group each have their own settings window. Two clocks can show two time zones; two weather items can show two cities.

To open an item's settings:

- Right-click the item on the dock and choose its settings entry: **Widget settings…** for a clock, a weather item, a widget and the tray, **Folder settings…** or **Group settings…** for a folder or a group.
- Or select its tile on the dock's Contents tab and click the button of the same name, or double-click the tile.

The settings are described in [Item settings](guide-settings.md#item-settings).

## Apps and pins

A pinned app stays on the dock whether it runs or not.

- **Click** an app to start it. If it is running, a click brings its window to the front, and a second click minimizes it. With several windows, clicks cycle through them.
- **Middle-click** starts a new copy of the app. The dock's Behaviour tab can make it minimize or close the app's windows instead.
- **Mouse wheel** over an app can switch between its windows, if you turn that on in the Behaviour tab.
- **Right-click** for **New window**, **Open file location**, **Close** for each window, **Customize icon**, and **Pin to dock** or **Unpin from dock**.
- **Drop files** on an app to open them with it.

While an app starts, its icon bounces until its window appears. A dot under the icon shows that it is running: brighter for the app in front, orange when the app wants your attention.

To give an app its own icon:

1. Right-click the app on the dock.
2. Open **Customize icon** and choose **Choose icon…** to pick an icon from a program or icon file, or **Choose image…** to use a picture.

**Reset icon** goes back to the app's own icon. The same choices are under **Customize icon ▾** on the Contents tab.

If a pinned program or shortcut no longer exists, the dock shows a placeholder icon instead. It never removes the pin by itself.

## Running apps

Apps that are running but not pinned appear where the dock's **Running apps** item is. Without that item, only pinned apps show their windows. You can drag running apps to reorder them for the session, or drop one among the pins to pin it there.

The settings are in the Running apps widget's own settings window: right-click a running app that is not pinned and choose **Running apps settings…** (the first entry under its name), or click **Running apps settings…** for the running apps on the Contents tab. There you also set the **Gap** between the running apps and the **Padding** beside them, their **Order** (as they appear, or by name), titles, thumbnails and notification badges. **Windows** chooses between **Stacked (one slot per app)** and **One slot per window**. With one slot per window, every window gets its own icon, like on the Windows taskbar: its name label shows the window title, and a click brings that window to the front. A minimized window keeps its slot.

### Previews

Rest the mouse on an app with two or more windows and a popup opens with a card for each window: its title and a live picture.

- Click a card to bring that window to the front.
- Click the × on a card, or middle-click it, to close that window.
- Move to another app to switch the popup to it.

Turn previews on or off, include apps with a single window, and set the delay on the dock's Behaviour tab under Window previews.

### Titles

**Titles under icons** writes the window title (or the app's name) under each icon, in one or two lines. On a left or right dock, **Titles on vertical docks** puts the title under the icon (the default) or beside it, toward the screen edge. Titles follow the windows as their titles change.

Use titles with **One slot per window** to tell your windows apart at a glance.

### Thumbnails

**Window thumbnails** turns each window's icon into a small picture of the window, with the app's icon in its corner.

- **Live (GPU capture)**: the pictures follow the windows as they change. It uses the graphics card a little, and only while the dock is visible.
- **Snapshots (every N seconds)**: a new picture every few seconds. It costs almost nothing in between.

**Tile length** sets how long each picture is along the dock (0: twice the icon size), and **Tile thickness** how thick it is across the dock (0: the icon size). A thicker tile makes the bar thicker; the icons stay centred beside the tiles. On a left or right dock the length runs down the bar: for landscape pictures there, make the thickness larger than the length (for example 100 and 180). Videos protected against copying show as black.

## Folders

A folder on the dock opens its contents in a popup, like a stack on a Mac.

To pin a folder, drag it from File Explorer onto the dock, or click **Folder…** in the Contents tab's drawer.

Working with a folder:

- Click the folder to open its popup. Click it again, or press Esc, to close it.
- Click a file to open it. Click a sub-folder to look inside it; the back arrow (or Backspace) goes up again.
- Type the start of a name to select it, and press Enter to open it.
- Drop files on the popup to move or copy them into the folder.
- Middle-click the folder to open it in File Explorer.

The popup shows a grid or a list. To choose, right-click the folder and open **Contents layout**, or open the folder's settings window and set **Contents layout**: **Automatic** (a grid up to 48 entries, a list for more), **Grid** or **List**.

The folder's settings window has the same view choice and an Icon card to give it a picture of your own.

## Groups

A group puts several shortcuts in one slot. Its icon shows the first four members. A click opens a popup with all of them.

To make a group:

1. Drag an app's icon onto another pinned app or folder.
2. Hold it there for a moment, until the target grows a little.
3. Let go.

To add another app, drag it onto the group. To take one out, drag it from the group's popup back onto the dock, or right-click it in the popup and choose **Remove from group** or **Move to dock**.

Right-click a group for **Show contents**, **Rename…**, **Contents layout**, **Customize icon**, **Group settings…**, **Ungroup (shortcuts stay pinned)** (its shortcuts become pins again) and **Unpin group and its shortcuts**. A group shows a running dot while any of its apps runs.

The group's settings window (right-click > **Group settings…**) has these cards:

- **Group**: its **Name** and **Contents layout**.
- **Icon**: see below.
- **Shortcuts in the group**: see below.

### Icons

The Icon card of a folder or a group has **Choose icon…**, **Choose image…** and **Reset icon**. Without an icon of your own, a folder shows the folder's icon and a group shows its first four members.

### Shortcuts in a group

The **Shortcuts in the group** card lists the group's members in order:

- **Add app…** and **Add folder…** add a member.
- **Remove** takes the selected member out.
- **Move up** and **Move down** change the order.

When the last member is removed, the group goes away.

## Separators and the Recycle Bin

A **separator** is a thin line between parts of the dock, for example between your apps and your widgets. Add as many as you like. Drag it to move it; right-click it and choose **Remove separator** to take it away.

The **Recycle Bin** shows whether it is empty or full, and its name label says how many items it holds.

- Click it to open the Recycle Bin.
- Drop files on it to delete them (you can undo that in File Explorer).
- Right-click it and choose **Empty Recycle Bin** to empty it.

## The clock

The clock shows the time and, if you like, the date and a label.

- Click the clock to open a month calendar. Use the arrows (or Page Up and Page Down) to change the month; click the month name to go back to today.
- With calendar feeds, the calendar also shows your events. See [Calendar and reminders](#calendar-and-reminders).

Each clock has its own settings window, with two tabs. The **Content** tab has three cards:

- **Time**: the **Time format** (for example `%H:%M` for 14:05 or `%I:%M %p` for 02:05 PM), **Show seconds** and the **Time zone**.
- **Text**: **Show the date under the time** and a **Label**, such as "Tokyo".
- **Click**: **Clicking the clock opens** the month calendar, an app or web address of your choice, or nothing.

The **Appearance** tab has the **Look** card: the **Face**. **Digital (built in)** is the usual look; the others are clock face files. **Open faces folder** opens the folder for your own faces. The clock's colours and font come from the dock's theme.

To show the time in another city:

1. Add a second clock from the Contents tab's drawer.
2. Open its settings.
3. Choose the city's zone in **Time zone** and type the city in **Label**.

For a round clock with hands, use the **Analog clock** widget.

## Weather

The **Weather (script)** widget shows the current conditions as a small picture and the temperature. A click opens a forecast: the next 24 hours and the next 7 days. The data comes from Open-Meteo, a free weather service. Older native Weather items are automatically replaced with this widget, keeping their place and settings.

To set the place:

1. Click the weather item. Without a place it shows a map pin, and a click opens its settings.
2. In **Find a place**, type the name of your town.
3. Pick it from the list.

The weather settings window has Content and Appearance tabs. Its place and updates controls include:

- **Place**: **Find a place**, the chosen **Place**, **Edit coordinates** (to type a latitude and longitude), the **Name on the dock** under the temperature, and the **Status** with **Refresh now**.
- **Updates**: **Units** (metric or imperial) and **Refresh every** (minutes).

Each weather widget can have its own place, so a dock can show two cities. Use **Refresh now** in its settings or the refresh button in its forecast popup to update its place.

The Appearance tab has the forecast on the dock and how it looks:

- **Forecast**: **Forecast on the dock** (none, today's high and low under the temperature, or several days) and **Days on the dock** (2 to 7).
- **Layout**: a preset for the slot. **Automatic** follows the dock's edge (a column per day on a bottom or top dock, a line per day on a left or right dock). **Strip** is a column per day beside the conditions. **Tiles** puts each day's name over its icon with the high over the low beside them, after the conditions on a bottom or top dock and stacked under them on a left or right dock: the easiest to read on a narrow dock. **List** stacks the icon over the temperature with a line per day under them. **Card** is for a desktop widget: big conditions with tiles under them. **Custom** shows three fields instead: where the icon goes (beside or above the temperature), what each day's cell is (a column, a tile, a line, or the icon with high/low), and whether the days go after or under the conditions.
- **Sizes and spacing** (in the Layout card): **Size of the whole slot** scales everything on a dock (also in the Contents inspector), **Size of the day cells** makes the forecast larger than the current conditions on its own, **Space between days** spreads the cells out, and **Space before the days** sets the gap after the current conditions. A thick dock reads best with the day cells at 150 % or more.
- **Text**: the condition's name under the temperature, the place, today's high and low, **Day names** (Mon, M or Monday), whether the first day says **Today**, and **High and low** (arrows, a slash or a space between them).
- **Icons**: **Icons** chooses WinDock's filled pictures, Lucide's line icons, or Meteocons, coloured icons that move (the sun's rays turn, clouds drift, rain falls); the motion runs in Windows' compositor, so it costs the dock nothing, and **Animated icons** turns it off (reduce motion does too). **Icon colours** keeps the yellow sun and blue rain or draws everything in the text colour, and **Compact** leaves only the icon.

Right-click the widget for the quickest changes: **Forecast on the dock**, **Layout** and **Icons** are in its menu.

## Calendar and reminders

WinDock reads calendars in iCal format: a web address, such as the secret address of a Google calendar or a published Outlook calendar, or an `.ics` file on your PC. It cannot sign in to an account.

To add a calendar, go to App settings > Calendar feeds and click **Add feed…**. See [Calendar feeds](guide-settings.md#calendar-feeds).

Your events then show up:

- **In the month calendar**: days with events get coloured dots. Click a day to see its events. Point at an event to see its time, place and description.
- **On the clock**: a small dot after the date while today has events.
- **In the Agenda widget**: the next event, with its time.
- **As reminders**: a banner beside the dock a few minutes before each event, with **Snooze 5 min** and **Dismiss**.

Reminders are off until you set **Default reminder** (or a feed's own reminder) to some minutes. All-day events have no reminder.

## The tray

The tray shows the notification area icons (the small icons at the right of the Windows taskbar) on the dock. It is off at first.

To show the tray icons on a dock:

1. Right-click the dock and choose **Add** > **Tray (notify icons)**. This also turns on the takeover.
2. Or add **System tray** from the Contents tab's drawer. This also turns on the takeover (the check box **Show the notification area icons in the dock** on App settings > Tray icons).
3. Or apply the **Windows 7** layout, which has the tray at the right end and turns on the takeover too (see [Make it look like Windows 7](guide-docks.md#make-it-look-like-windows-7)).

Clicks on the icons go to their apps as they would on the taskbar: a right-click opens the app's own menu. **Ctrl+right-click** an icon opens WinDock's tray menu: hide it from the grid, pin it to the dock, or open the tray settings.

When there are more icons than cells, the last cell is an arrow that opens a drawer with every icon.

Windows' own items (network, battery, language, its clock and volume) stay with Windows. The Volume, Battery and clock widgets cover them.

To give the tray a small dock of its own at the bottom right, click **Put the tray in its own dock** on App settings > Tray icons.

### The grid

The tray item's settings window starts with **The grid**:

- **Rows**: stacked top to bottom, up to 20.
- **Columns**: left to right, up to 20.
- **Icon size**: 16, 20 or 24.

Rows and columns are as you see them on screen, whichever edge the dock is on. Across the bar (rows on a bottom or top dock, columns on a left or right one) two cells always fit, shrunk to half an icon each. More fit only when the bar is thick enough for them at full size: turn on **Fixed thickness** on Dock settings > Position, or show titles beside the icons. Cells that do not fit are left out; the drawer still has every icon.

### Icons in the grid

This card lists every icon in the grid's order. Clear an icon's check box to keep it only in the drawer. **Move up** and **Move down** change the order. You can also drag icons within the grid on the dock, or drag one out onto the dock to pin it there as a full-size icon.

### Takeover

**Reset to Explorer** hands every icon back to Windows and turns the takeover off, on every dock. Turn it on again on App settings > Tray icons.

## Notifications

WinDock can read the notifications in the Windows notification centre. Windows must allow it: Settings > Privacy & security > Notifications > "Let apps access your notifications".

The **notification centre** is a bell on the dock with the number of unread notifications. To add it, click or drag **Notification centre** in the drawer (see [Adding items](#adding-items)).

- Click the bell to see your notifications, newest first.
- Click a notification to open its app.
- Click the × on a notification, or press Delete, to dismiss it. **Clear all** dismisses every one.

**Badges** put the number of unread notifications on each app's icon. Turn them on with **Notification badges on app icons** on the dock's Behaviour tab. To keep an app from ever showing a badge, clear it in App settings > Notifications > Badges per app.

An app's notifications count as read when you dismiss them in Windows, or when you open the app from the dock.

## Widgets

Widgets are small programs that live in a slot of the dock or on the desktop. Each one runs safely on its own: a widget that misbehaves is stopped without affecting the dock.

### Built-in widgets

WinDock comes with these widgets:

- **Analog clock**: a clock with hands and five faces, optionally with a second hand, the date and a time zone. It grows a little while the pointer is over it.
- **Digital clock (script)**: a scripted copy of the dock's own clock, with a date format of your own. Its **Layout** "Taskbar" writes the time over the date in two lines of the same size, as the Windows 7 taskbar clock (for example the time format `%#I:%M %p` over the date format `%#m/%#d/%Y`: 2:05 PM over 11/6/2026).
- **Calendar page**: today's date as a small calendar page. A click opens the month calendar.
- **Agenda**: your next calendar event. A click shows the next events.
- **Weather (script)**: the weather with more choices, such as a forecast of several days on the dock.
- **Volume**: the sound level. The wheel changes it, a middle-click mutes, a click opens a slider and the output devices.
- **Now playing**: the song or video that is playing, with previous, play / pause and next.
- **Notes**: a sticky note. A click opens a small editor.
- **System monitor**: one measurement, such as processor, memory, graphics, disk, network or battery, as a graph, a bar, a dial or a number.
- **System overview**: processor, memory, graphics, disk and network at once.
- **Battery**: the battery level, whether it charges and the time left.
- **Stocks**: quotes for the ticker symbols you list (stocks, ETFs, forex and crypto as Finnhub spells them), one symbol at a time or all of them, with the change since the previous close in green or red. It needs a free API key from finnhub.io, entered under Widget settings > Data. A click opens the day's figures; a click on a symbol there opens it on Yahoo Finance.
- **Photo frame**: a picture in a frame, or a folder of pictures as a slideshow. Choose the picture or the folder with the Browse… button in its settings (Content > Picture). A slideshow goes by name, by date or shuffled, waits the time you set on each picture, and changes with a fade, a slide from a side, or at once. Under Appearance pick the frame's size on a dock and its shape (the picture's own, square, 4:3, 16:9, a portrait ratio, or your own width and height; on the desktop the window is the frame), whether a picture of another shape is cropped to fill the frame or fits inside it, the border (none, a thin line, a mat, a Polaroid band), rounded corners and the drop shadow. A click shows the next picture (or opens the picture), a middle click opens it, and the wheel steps through a slideshow.
- **Start** and **Search**: open the Start menu and Windows Search. Start shows the Windows logo, your own icon or the **Windows 7 orb**, a glossy sphere with the four-colour flag that lights up under the pointer and darkens while pressed. Its icon size 0 is automatic: the orb then fills the slot. The Start menu opens next to the button you clicked: above it on a bottom dock, beside it on a side dock. Its **Menu position** setting (Content) can put it back where Windows puts it; the Windows key always opens it at its usual place.
- **Show desktop**: the narrow strip at the end of the Windows 7 taskbar. On a full-width dock it is as tall as the bar and, as the last item, sits right against the screen's end. A click shows the desktop (Win+D; a second click brings the windows back) or, if you choose, minimizes every window (Win+M). Its style can also be a small desktop glyph, for other themes. Hovering it does not make the windows transparent (Aero Peek is not available).
- **Notification bell**: a bell like the notification centre, with more choices.
- **Hello**: a tiny example widget.

The System monitor can also show any sensor of HWiNFO (temperatures, fans, power) while HWiNFO runs with its shared memory turned on. See [Performance and memory](troubleshooting.md#performance-and-memory).

### Widget settings

Every widget on a dock has its own settings window. To open it, right-click the widget and choose **Widget settings…**, or select it on the Contents tab and click its settings button.

Every widget's window has the same structure, in two tabs:

- **Content**: what the widget shows and does: its place, feeds, metric or data choices, time zone, labels, and what a click or the wheel does.
- **Appearance**: how it looks: its style, face, colours, sizes and motion.

A widget whose settings are all of one kind (Hello, Start, Search) has one page and no tabs. Inside a tab the settings are grouped in cards. Each setting has a short hint under it; rest the mouse on it for the full explanation. Some settings appear only when another one has a certain value.

A colour setting shows a small swatch of the colour beside its box. Click the swatch to choose a colour in the Windows colour dialog, or type the colour into the box as #AARRGGBB (the first pair is the opacity: FF is solid). Picking a colour keeps the opacity you had; a colour that was fully transparent (many widgets' way of saying "use the theme's colour") becomes solid. The same swatch appears in the Contents tab for a widget's everyday settings.

The footer, under both tabs, has **Reset to factory defaults…** (back to the widget's own defaults, after asking) and **Console…** (the widget's log and errors).

A widget with a place, such as Weather (script), has a **Find a place** box. Without a place of its own it shows the default place; the hint under **Place** says which. Once you choose a place for it, a **↺** beside the place and **Reset to app default** in the footer make it show the shared place again.

### Widgets on the desktop

A widget can also live on the desktop in a window of its own, moved and sized freely. Drag its tile from the drawer onto the desktop, press the small monitor button on its tile, or click **Add to the desktop** in App settings > Widget library. See [Standalone widgets](guide-docks.md#standalone-widgets).

A widget already on the dock can move to the desktop too: drag it up and off the dock (it fades a little when you are far enough) and let go. It leaves the dock and appears on the desktop under the pointer, with the same settings. Press **Esc** before you let go to cancel, or choose **Undo** in the dock menu afterwards to put it back. This works for script widgets such as Hello, Digital clock or Weather (script), not for the dock's own Clock, Weather, Tray and Notification centre items, the running apps, or a locked dock; a widget made to need a dock stays on it.

To put a desktop widget back on a dock, drag its window onto the dock's bar: a marker shows where it will go between the items, and when you let go it joins the dock there and its window closes, with the same settings. Let go anywhere else and it just moves; press **Esc** during the drag to put it back where it was. Or right-click the widget and choose **Move to dock**, then the dock: it is added at the end. **Undo** in the dock menu brings the window back. A locked dock does not take it (its entry in the menu is greyed), and a dock that is hidden at its edge must be showing for the drop to land.

### Your own widgets

A widget is a folder with a small description file and a JavaScript file. You can write your own, or change a copy of a built-in one, without any tools.

- App settings > Widget library > **More ▾** > **Add from folder…** installs a widget from a folder.
- **Export built-in to folder** makes an editable copy of a built-in widget.
- **Open widgets folder** opens the folder where your widgets live.
- **Console…** shows each widget's log and errors.

Everything about writing widgets is in the [widget developer reference](widgets.md).
